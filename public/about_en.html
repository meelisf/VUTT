Project Description

VUTT (Early Modern Text Workspace) is a web application for viewing and editing transcriptions of historical documents. The application displays scanned document images and LLM-recognized text side-by-side, allowing for text correction and annotation.

The project's goal is to make available and searchable texts from the Early Modern University of Tartu printing house (disputations, orations, etc.).

Corpus

The corpus brings together material connected with early modern Livonia and Estonia (regardless of place of printing) — disputations, orations, etc. The material is organized into collections:

  • Academia Gustaviana (1632–1665)
  • Academia Gustavo-Carolina (1690–1710)
  • Funeral prints — a thematic collection of funeral publications
  • Pedagogical-Philological Seminar

The texts originate mostly from the digitized collections of the UT Library and are largely based on copies of publications collected during the compilation of Ene-Lille Jaanson, comp., Druckerei der Universität Dorpat 1632-1710: Geschichte und Bibliographie der Druckschriften (Tartu University Library, 2000).

Recognition Models

VUTT uses two page-based recognition models: one for printed works and one for handwritten texts.

Print model

The print model is based on Qwen3.5-8B and has been fine-tuned in two stages:

  • First stage: on ~1500 pages of transcribed printed text originating from various databases (including inspiration from data at zenodo.org/records/15764161).
  • Second stage: on pages already edited to completion in VUTT, so that the model learns the VUTT markup.

Handwriting model

For handwritten texts there is a separate model, trained on public handwriting datasets (~16,600 pages in total, mainly 14th–19th century German and Swedish chancery and Kurrent hands). Datasets used:

  • kurrent_xix — German, 19th c., ~8,000 pp. (CC BY 4.0)1
  • aaeb_xiv_xvii — German, 14th–17th c., ~1,990 pp. (CC BY 4.0)2
  • bullinger_autoren — German/Latin, 16th c., ~1,840 pp. (CC BY 4.0)3
  • bergskollegium_rel_seg — Swedish/German, 17th–18th c., ~1,440 pp.4
  • hanse_kurrent_xvi — German, 16th c., ~1,140 pp. (MIT)5
  • svea_hovratt_seg — Swedish, 17th–18th c., ~850 pp.6
  • trolldomskommissionen_seg — Swedish, 17th c., ~760 pp.7
  • krigshovrattens_seg — Swedish, 17th–18th c., ~340 pp.8
  • jonkopings_seg — Swedish, 17th–18th c., ~57 pp.9
  • bergskollegium_adv_seg — Swedish/German, 17th–18th c., ~53 pp.10
  • gota_hovratt_seg — Swedish, 17th–18th c., ~51 pp.11
  • koenigsfelden_adhr — German, 19th c., ~34 pp. (CC BY 4.0)12
  • dresdner_hofdiarium_1673 — German, 1673, ~20 pp. (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)13

Numbers

  • ca 1,260 works
  • ca 20,500 pages
  • Languages: Latin, German, Swedish, Greek, Hebrew

Technology

  • Frontend: React + TypeScript + Tailwind CSS
  • Search Engine: Meilisearch
  • OCR: fine-tuned Qwen3.5-8B model (print) and a separate handwriting model

VUTT is developed at UT Library. The logo was suggested by Rahel Toomik and comes from the decorative frame of the title page of the sermon Een kort och enfaldigh Lijkpredikan ... Dorpt: J. Vogel, 1642.
